TEACHING TOOLS · PHILOSOPHY · SPANISH

Philosophy, made walkable

An argument is something a student should have to use, not something to memorise.

A complete unit on Descartes' Meditations in four forms of the same text: five scrollytelling lessons, a 3D simulation of the wax experiment, an argument game where the evil demon prosecutes the cogito, and El Edificio — a six-floor escape room whose doors only open if you use the argument rather than recall it. Plus standalone lessons on Plato's cave and the road from feudalism to modernity. Art, voice-over and puzzles ship inside the files, so a school machine with no network still runs them.

TEACHING TOOLS · ENGLISH · B1–B1+

English that marks itself

Feedback that arrives while the student still cares about it.

Around thirty pages of practice, all built on one rule: the page checks every answer on the spot, says what went wrong, and lets the student retry. Two multi-stop grammar quests (irregular past simple, present perfect), exam-format Use of English sessions, classroom games and the tests that mark the same content, a reading trivia round, and The Imaginarium Machine — a creative-writing app that deals out prompts and then gets out of the way. No accounts, no logins, nothing about the student leaves the browser.

AI AGENT · GOVERNMENT DATA

EHOBA

A monthly 53-hotel survey turned into a rule-bound processing agent.

EHOBA processes Buenos Aires's monthly hotel occupancy survey for IDECBA, validates submissions against fixed consistency rules, and follows a documented eight-step correction order. I built it end to end with Claude Code, including the desktop UI, processing pipeline, and design system. The same habit as the classroom work, on higher stakes: the rules are written down first, and the tool is held to them.

How I work

I've worked with language models since 2022 — prompting them, testing their reasoning, watching where they fail.

I build with them on one condition: they extend human judgment, they don't stand in for it. Bias, gaps, and confident errors are the interesting part — the places where a philosopher's training earns its keep. That criterion is the whole point.
